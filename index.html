Basics

What the number means

Every score is the share of graduating seniors who gave one of the top two answers.

  • Dashboards 1 to 3 (satisfaction): Satisfied or Very Satisfied, out of four options.
  • Dashboard 4 (skills): Quite a bit or Very much, out of four options.

So "84%" means 84 out of 100 responding seniors picked one of the top two boxes. Higher is better. It is not an average score and not a grade.

Percentage points, not percent

If your department is at 63% and UCLA is at 72%, the gap is 9 percentage points, not 9 percent. Writing it as "9% lower" is wrong and will get corrected in a meeting. Say "9 points below the UCLA average."

The grey line

Every trend panel has two lines:

Blue
Your program. Whatever you selected in the filters.
Grey
All UCLA seniors. The campus-wide figure for that same question and year.

The grey line answers "is 84% good?" — the first thing anyone asks, and the thing a single number can never tell you.

Why lines have gaps

If fewer than 11 students answered a question in a given year, we do not publish the result. This is a privacy requirement (FERPA), not missing data. With very small groups, a published percentage can identify individual students.

You will see this most in the Minor dashboard, where fewer students answer. It is normal and it is not a bug.

Select at least 3 graduating classes

These dashboards are built to show change over time. With one or two years selected, the chart cannot show direction, and the panel titles may not fit.

One thing to keep in mind about 2020 and 2021

Nearly every item dipped during the remote-instruction years and then recovered. That dip sits in the middle of every line. If you compare only 2021 to 2025, you will overstate improvement. The full line makes this visible, which is another reason to keep all seven years on.

Using the filters

The controls run in this order:

  1. Compare by. The level you want: All UCLA, school, division, department, or major.
  2. Choose one. The specific unit.
  3. Graduating class. Which years (keep 3 or more).
  4. Topic. Optional; narrows to 2 or 3 related questions.

Set 1 and 2 first. They define who you are looking at. Everything else adjusts what you see about them.
